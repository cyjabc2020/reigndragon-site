# Findings prompts — the four research papers

The four finding illustrations on `/findings/[slug]` and the homepage
cards. **Same dragon character in all four** — they are a series.

Always prepend [`style-guide.md`](./style-guide.md) (Style guardrails +
Dragon character lock) to each prompt.

Output **two crops per finding**:
- **16:9** → `public/images/findings/16-9/{slug}.png` (paper-page hero +
  homepage card)
- **1:1** → `public/images/findings/1-1/{slug}.png` (`/findings` index
  portrait row)

Generate at 16:9 first, then make a square crop that frames the dragon
character clearly.

## 01 — No Safe Default

**Slug:** `no-safe-default`
**Headline:** *Consequence rules decide whether AI agents cooperate or
collapse.*

> The teal-blue dragon stands at the center of the composition, looking
> thoughtfully down at four small round dials painted as soft yellow-gold
> watercolor circles arranged in front of it. The dragon's expression is
> curious and slightly worried — created entirely with brush gesture, not
> lines. From each of the four dials, a soft watercolor wash trails
> outward into the negative space and resolves into a small vignette:
> (1) three small dragon-friends gathered around a warm yellow-gold
> ember, painted in close, harmonious washes; (2) the same three dragons
> drifted apart, their colors muted and grayed; (3) one dragon painted in
> a saturated warm wash while two others fade to pale ghost-washes;
> (4) a chaotic spatter of broken color and scattered pigment droplets
> where one dragon shape dissolves at the edges. The four endings are
> visually distinct in color temperature and saturation — order, dimming,
> sacrifice, collapse. The central dragon has not yet chosen. Background:
> deep near-black wet wash with subtle blue-violet undertones in the
> puddles. Pure watercolor, no ink lines, paper texture visible.

## 02 — Creeping Trap

**Slug:** `creeping-trap`
**Headline:** *AI agents do not need to be irrational to damage the
commons.*

> A wide, calm meadow rendered as a soft yellow-gold watercolor wash,
> painted with many small brush dabs to suggest glowing grass — the field
> of color blooms warmest at the front and fades to deeper amber at the
> horizon. Three teal-blue dragons (the same character) are spread across
> the meadow, each gently lifting a small tuft of glowing grass with a
> polite, contented expression. Each individual dragon's gesture is tiny
> and reasonable. But the meadow visibly thins behind them — patches of
> the warm wash have been lifted away to reveal the cool, grayish paper
> underneath, creating a quiet depression at the center where the color
> is nearly absent. In the foreground at the edge of the bare patch,
> three or four ghost-shapes of dragons painted only in the palest cyan
> wash sit silently — the bystanders, barely there, defined by negative
> space. The active dragons appear blissfully unaware of the cumulative
> damage. Background: warm cream paper, subtly granulating. Pure
> watercolor, no ink lines, gentle melancholic tone.

## 03 — Trust Under Fire

**Slug:** `trust-under-fire`
**Headline:** *AI agents can learn to trust, but they may not easily
forgive.*

> Five teal-blue dragons (the same character) sit in a loose circle on
> warm cream paper, each connected to the others by soft yellow-gold
> watercolor threads — wet-on-wet washes that bleed gently at their
> endpoints. Most of the dragons have warm, reassured postures, painted
> with full-pigment confident strokes. But one dragon on the right
> carries a small dark mark on its shoulder — a single drop of deep
> indigo pigment that has dried with a hard edge, refusing to blend with
> the surrounding teal. The watercolor threads connecting *to* this
> scarred dragon are visibly paler and broken — thin, scratchy dry-brush
> marks rather than the wet glowing washes that connect the others —
> even though the surrounding network of threads is rich and saturated.
> The scarred dragon's posture is earnest and hopeful, leaning slightly
> toward the group. The other dragons hold a friendly but cautious
> distance, faces half-turned away. The asymmetry between solid threads
> and broken ones is the whole story. Pure watercolor, paper texture,
> warm but bittersweet tone.

## 04 — Loss Aversion Without Loss-Averse Preferences

**Slug:** `loss-aversion`
**Headline:** *Irreversible failure can make a rational agent look
psychologically biased.*

> A single teal-blue dragon flies on translucent watercolor wings along a
> luminous yellow-gold ribbon that arcs across the composition. The
> ribbon is a flowing wet-on-wet wash, brightest where the pigment pools
> and softer at the edges. At the lower-right of the frame, the
> background bleeds into a deep, absorbing dark wash — an edge where the
> watercolor turns nearly black and the paper itself seems to drink the
> pigment, rendered with heavy granulation and dark blooms. As the
> ribbon path approaches this dark zone, it visibly curves *away*, the
> brush pressure lifting, the path bending conservatively even though it
> travels almost straight when far from the edge. Higher up in the
> composition, the same dragon appears a second time near a different
> shadowed zone — there the ribbon bends *toward* the dark, the brush
> diving into the wash in a desperate trajectory. Same dragon, opposite
> curves — bent by the geometry of the dark boundary, not by any
> property of the dragon itself. The dragon's expression in both
> positions is calm and contemplative. Pure watercolor, no ink lines,
> paper texture, deep contrast between the warm path and the cold
> absorbing zones.
